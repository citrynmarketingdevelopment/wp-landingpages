The Service

What tenant improvements and build-outs involve

Tenant improvement work covers the changes needed to make a leased commercial space usable for a specific tenant: partitions and layout, finishes, lighting and power, and the mechanical and plumbing changes the new use requires. Scope is often shaped as much by the lease and the landlord's requirements as by the tenant's own wish list.

We work through those constraints early so the scope reflects what is actually approvable, then phase construction to limit disruption to the building and its other occupants. If you own the property rather than lease it, commercial remodeling and renovations is the closer fit, and commercial general contracting and project management covers the oversight side in more detail.

When a tenant improvement makes sense

Tenant improvement work usually starts with a lease and a space that does not yet match the business going into it. Common situations include:

  • You have signed on shell or dated spaceThe location works but the suite needs building out before you can trade from it.
  • The layout does not fit operationsThe square footage is right but the walls are in the wrong places for how you work.
  • You are renewing and updatingStaying put but taking the opportunity to bring the space up to standard.
  • You are expanding into adjacent spaceTaking on a neighboring suite and needing the two combined into one workable footprint.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is usually included in a tenant improvement scope?

Typically partitions and layout changes, ceilings and flooring, paint and finishes, doors and hardware, and the lighting, power, and mechanical changes the new use requires. The exact scope depends on the condition of the space and what the lease makes the tenant responsible for.

Who coordinates between the landlord and the tenant?

We do, on the construction side. Landlords commonly set requirements around approved plans, working hours, access, insurance, and how the work is left. We confirm those up front and work to them so approvals do not become the thing holding up your opening date.

Do tenant improvements need plans and permits?

Most do, particularly where partitions, electrical, or mechanical work is involved. For work that requires it, plans, permits, and inspections are coordinated into the schedule alongside any landlord approvals.

Can you work in a building that is still occupied?

Yes, and most tenant improvements happen in exactly that situation. Work is phased and scheduled around the building's operating hours where possible, with attention to noise, dust, access, and shared areas that other tenants still rely on.

How does phased construction work on a build-out?

We break the work into stages so trades follow each other cleanly and disruption to the building is contained. On larger suites this can also mean releasing finished areas in sequence, so you can begin moving in while the remaining scope is completed.

How do office and retail build-outs differ?

Office work concentrates on layout, acoustics, lighting, and data and power distribution. Retail focuses more on the customer-facing environment, storefront and display requirements, and back of house. Retail scope also tends to be shaped more tightly by landlord and centre standards.

What happens at closeout?

Final inspections are completed, the work is checked against the agreed scope, and a punch list is documented and worked through before handover, so you are not still chasing snags after you have moved in.

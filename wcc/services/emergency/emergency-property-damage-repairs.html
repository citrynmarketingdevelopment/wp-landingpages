Safety First

Safety and access after property damage

Before anyone re-enters a damaged property, some basics apply:

  • Keep clear of compromised structureStay away from anything leaning, sagging, or holding weight it was not built for.
  • Assume utilities may be affectedImpact and failures can compromise electrical and other services. Treat the area with caution.
  • Limit access to the damaged areaKeep people out until the property has been assessed and secured.
  • Document the sceneIf it is safe, photograph the damage before anything is moved or cleared.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What qualifies as emergency property damage?

Any sudden damage that leaves a building compromised or unsecured: vehicle impact, sudden failures, break-in damage, and similar events. If the property needs to be made safe and secured before normal repairs can even be planned, it belongs here.

Do you respond to both residential and commercial properties?

Yes. We handle urgent property damage for both homes and businesses. The response follows the same shape either way: assess, secure, stabilize, document, and plan the reconstruction.

Do you provide board-up and temporary containment?

Yes. Board-up and containment are how we secure a compromised property against further loss, closing openings and protecting damaged areas until permanent repairs are made.

Will I have a record of the damage and the repair scope?

Yes. We document the damage and the repair scope clearly, so you have a record of what happened and what the reconstruction involves for your own files and for anyone you need to report to.

Is it safe to go back into the damaged area?

Not until it has been assessed and secured. Keep clear of compromised structure, assume utilities in the area may be affected, and limit access until we have made the property safe. Damaged property can be rebuilt, so do not take a risk to re-enter it.

What does the permanent reconstruction involve?

Once the property is secure, we plan and complete the construction repairs the damage requires, from stabilized areas through to finished reconstruction, coordinated by the same contractor that secured the site.
